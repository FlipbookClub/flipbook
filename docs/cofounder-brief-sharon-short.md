# Flipbook — An invitation

**For:** Sharon "Ronnie" Ariyo-Adeoye
**From:** Victory Moks & Ayodeji
**Role:** Cofounder — Brand, Story & Community

---

Ronnie,

You say the biggest mistake African founders make is failing to build locally while positioning globally. That's the mistake we're currently making, and it's why we're writing.

**What we've built.** A reading app where people read the same book together — reactions and comments land in the margins, on the exact paragraph, as you read. 185 signups. **16 communities formed with zero marketing and nobody in the company owning growth.** Live on TestFlight and the Play Console. Real backend, offline reading, a native highlighting layer we built ourselves.

**What we got wrong.** We've been calling ourselves a library for African readers. That describes who it's for, not where it's from — and it tells a reader in Jakarta she isn't invited, when she is.

Because Flipbook is two things with two different ceilings. The **library** is licensed, territory-bound, slow — it grows deal by deal, starting with African independent authors and the classics. The **room** — reading together, the margins, the shared progress — needs no licence from anyone. It works identically in Lagos, Lisbon, Jakarta, Lima. Tonight.

And that ground is vacant. Kindle owns solo reading; we'd lose that fight. **Nobody owns reading together.** Goodreads sat on it for fifteen years and let it rot. So:

> **Kindle is where you read. Flipbook is where you read together.**

We want that to become the phrase readers everywhere use — the app a book club in any city assumes, the way they assume WhatsApp for the group chat. We're 185 users in. We know how that sounds. We also think the mechanism that made 16 communities appear without us trying doesn't care what country it's in.

---

## Why you

**Your own framework diagnoses us.** Build locally, position globally, don't sand off what made you interesting. You've spent three years on that question for individuals. It's now our central product question and we don't have the answer.

**You can earn attention.** Four publications covered a three-year-old agency. You didn't buy that. We need the world to notice a reading app from Lagos — you've already done the harder version for yourself.

**You build real community.** HERcelerate isn't a hashtag. You sponsored a woman's lash-tech training, found out mid-programme she couldn't read or write, and enrolled her in adult education. That instinct is the one our retention story depends on.

**You've been a founder.** No capital, no plan, a Canva flyer. We don't want an executive.

---

## What you'd own

**The story.** Our public voice and narrative. The hard part: how does a brand stay unmistakably from Lagos while being desirable to a reader in Jakarta? Too cautious and we're a regional app with a ceiling. Too ambitious and we're generic. We don't know. You might.

**The audience.** Acquisition, activation, retention — plus the judgment call on sequencing. The room scales free; the library doesn't. Where we push, in what order, and the discipline not to chase five markets before winning one.

**The community.** Sixteen communities appeared and nobody has asked *why*. Understood and made repeatable, that's our most valuable asset — it's the part that crosses borders without a rights conversation. Start with Oyinadé Balógun of Lumee Book Club, our most engaged tester, who's already offered to bring others.

You'd inherit craft, not a blank page: full design system, voice guide, documented anti-patterns. Challenge all of it.

---

## Honestly

**No salary.** None. We're paying for servers out of our own pockets. So we can't ask you to turn down paid work, can't ask for exclusivity, and can't ask you to put Lenora second. Those asks belong to companies that can pay.

**What we offer instead:** real vested equity, founding status with your name on it from the start, complete creative authority over brand, and two people who ship weekly instead of talking about it. When we launch, it's your name on that story — fuel for Lenora, not competition with it. Not offered as payment; just true.

**It's early and hard.** 185 users. No revenue. We've pivoted once and might again. The retention problem is genuinely unsolved. The global ambition is a risk as much as an opportunity — companies die serving everywhere before winning somewhere. Our view is global architecture, local go-to-market. If you think that's wrong, tell us in week one.

**We argue.** Victory has strong opinions about brand. Ayodeji about anything touching revenue. You'd need to hold your ground. We want that.

**On hours.** Not the real question. The real question is whether it lands on your desk when a launch falls flat. What we need: at the weekly review most weeks, reachable within a day, genuinely present through launch. Not forty hours at a desk. For what it's worth, we're both still balancing this too.

**On equity.** Cofounder equity — real and properly papered. Four-year vest, one-year cliff, acceleration on acquisition, reviewed at twelve months, lawyered before you start. We've deliberately left the number out: it should follow the shape, and the shape is a conversation with you, not a figure handed to you.

## Next

1. Tell us where we're wrong. Before you say yes, not after.
2. A long conversation, not an interview.
3. **Two weeks building one real thing together** — we'd suggest the positioning. Not an audition; peers finding out if they work well together. Nobody's paid, us included. If we all say yes, that time starts your vesting.
4. Then we agree the shape and the number, and paper it.

---

You've said there's no bad story, only bad translation.

Flipbook has a good story and we've been telling it badly — worse, telling it small. We described a Lagos reading app when we're building the room where the world reads together, and we couldn't see the difference until recently.

You've spent three years teaching people to be from somewhere and legible everywhere.

Come do it for something bigger than a profile.

— Victory & Ayodeji
