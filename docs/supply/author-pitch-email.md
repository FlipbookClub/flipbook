# Flipbook — Indie-Author Pitch Email

**Owner:** Moks
**Status:** v1 draft, July 15 2026
**Use:** Warm and semi-warm outreach to Nigerian and African indie authors + small publishers. First supply-side push in the 90-day plan to first paying reader.
**Voice:** Founder writing personally, not marketing. First-person. Short. Concrete.

---

## The email

**Subject line options (pick per-recipient):**

- `Would love to feature [Book Title] on Flipbook`
- `Building a home for indie African authors — is your work in?`
- `A rental home for [Book Title]` *(only when you've read it)*

---

**Body (≤150 words):**

> Hi [First name],
>
> I'm Moks — I'm building **Flipbook**, a reading app out of Lagos where readers rent books instead of buying them. Think Netflix for books, with a real community layer.
>
> I've been reading [Book Title / your work on X] and I'd love to feature it on Flipbook when we open the catalog this September.
>
> Here's what I'm offering, plainly:
>
> - **70/30 rev-share** in your favor on every rental.
> - **No upfront money required** and **no exclusivity** — you keep publishing everywhere else.
> - Your book rents for **₦1,500–₦2,500** per reader for 4 weeks. You get 70% of that, paid monthly.
> - A real shot at readers Amazon doesn't show you to — plus a community forming around your book inside the app.
>
> Would you be open to a 15-minute call this week? I'll walk you through the app, the numbers, and the one-page terms — you decide from there.
>
> — Moks

## Sending notes

- **Send from a real address** — `moks@getflipbook.com`, never `hello@` or `no-reply@`. This is a founder-to-founder pitch, not a mailing list.
- **Personalize the first two lines every time.** The Book Title reference is what makes this not-spam. If you haven't read at least one thing they've written, don't send.
- **One CTA only.** The 15-minute call. Don't offer the term sheet as an attachment — that's the reason for the call.
- **Follow up once after 5 days**, then let it go. If they didn't reply to the first, a third email is friction, not persistence.

## Reply flows

**If they say "tell me more before a call":** Send the one-page term sheet (docs/supply/author-term-sheet.md) as PDF. Keep the note above it to 3 sentences: *"Attached is the one-pager. All the numbers are in there. Reply with any questions — happy to jump on a call whenever works."*

**If they say "yes to a call":** Send a Cal.com link (or WhatsApp voice-note time slot). Do the call in 15 minutes. End with: *"Send me the ISBN or EPUB when you're ready and I'll add you to the September launch cohort."*

**If they say "how many readers do you have?":** Be honest. *"We're at [N] waitlist signups and [N] active clubs in the current beta. The rental catalog opens in September. You'd be in the founding-author cohort — first thirty in, featured placement in the launch shelf."*

**If they say "not right now":** *"Totally understood. Mind if I circle back after we've launched? Some of the founding-cohort authors will earn out early and I'd love to have real numbers to bring back to you."*

---

## Personalization variables

For every author on the target list, capture:

- First name
- Book title (or Substack / IG series name) you've actually engaged with
- One specific line of praise you can drop *without* it being generic
- Contact vector (email preferred, DM as fallback, WhatsApp if warm intro)
- Warmth signal: cold / warm intro possible / mutual connection

Never send this email without the second and third items filled in. A blank there means you're pitching a stranger with a form letter — which is the opposite of what makes this work.
