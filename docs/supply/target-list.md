**Owner:** Moks

**Purpose:** Names, entities, and communities to pitch or partner with as we assemble the founding 30 authors on Flipbook. Living doc — validate contacts before sending, add and cross off as the outreach runs.

---

## 0. How to use this list

- Entries are grouped by tier (publishers → indie authors → community allies → ecosystem gateways). Read Tier 1 and Tier 2 as *pitch targets*; Tier 3 as *amplifiers* (they help you find and reach authors, but you don't pitch them to license their books); Tier 4 as *hunting grounds* to find more names beyond this seed list.
- Every entry has a **confidence** flag. `Solid` = named entity I'm confident exists and is relevant. `Verify` = named entity but you should check current status (still active? still indie? still accepting queries?) before sending. `Direction only` = pointer to a category / platform rather than a specific pitch target.
- **Never send the pitch email without doing two things per entry:** verify the contact vector (email address or DM handle), and confirm you've engaged with at least one specific piece of their work so the personal hook isn't generic.
- The list is intentionally overweight Nigerian voices — that's the beachhead. Ghanaian, Kenyan, and South African indies come in the second wave.

## 4. Tier 4 — Ecosystem gateways *(hunting grounds, not entries)*

Where to *find* more names beyond this seed list.

- **Okada Books** (okadabooks.com) — the Nigerian indie e-book platform. Their bestseller charts across romance, spec-fic, non-fiction, and Christian fiction are your literal hunting list. Every author with 500+ readers on Okada is a potential Flipbook founder.
- **Selar** (selar.co) — Nigerian digital-product marketplace. Many indie authors sell PDFs and short e-books here. Slightly less curated than Okada but broader.
- **Amazon KDP African-author charts** — sort by Nigerian, Kenyan, Ghanaian, South African tags. Authors publishing there are already comfortable with rev-share.
- **Substack Africa / Nigeria discovery** — serialized fiction and long-form is where the newest indie voices live.
- **Ankara Press** (imprint of Cassava Republic focused on African romance) — direct catalog worth checking for author names.
- **Bakwa Magazine (Cameroon), Doek! (Namibia), Lolwe (pan-African)** — literary magazines that publish the freshest indie short fiction.
- **The Kalahari Review, Adda (Commonwealth Writers), Africa in Dialogue** — same, editorial voices with connections.

Spend 90 minutes here at pitch-list-building time and you'll add 15–20 verified names to the target list.

## 5. Who's NOT on this list, and why

Being explicit so you don't waste a week trying:

- **Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie, Ayòbámi Adébáyò, Oyinkan Braithwaite, Nnedi Okorafor, Tade Thompson, Teju Cole, Helon Habila.** All represented by literary agents at CAA, WME, Curtis Brown, or similar. Their backlist rights are locked with big publishers. Pitching them directly at this stage is a waste of energy — you go through their agents once Flipbook has 5,000+ Pro subscribers and can walk into a meeting with real numbers.
- **Big-Five-published African authors generally.** Same reason. Come back at scale.
- **Poetry-only authors.** Not because poetry doesn't matter — because rental economics don't math out on a 40-page poetry collection at the bands we set. Include them later as free Specials once you have a catalog.
- **Academic authors and textbooks.** Wrong reader, wrong price point, wrong distribution logic. Different beachhead entirely.

## 8. Honest caveats

- **Where I've written specific author or publisher names, I'm drawing from training data.** People move, publishers close, indie authors go trad and back again. Every entry above should be checked against current reality before you send.
- **Where I've written a Twitter/IG handle, don't take my punctuation as gospel.** Handles get changed, dropped, migrated. Search the name and cross-check before sending a DM.
- **Some entries marked "Solid"** are people I know exist and know are relevant — but that doesn't guarantee they're accepting queries this week. Assume 30-40% of "Solid" entries will bounce for reasons unrelated to your pitch (out of country, on deadline, dealing with a family thing).
- **This list is deliberately Nigeria-heavy.** That's the beachhead. The second-wave list (weeks 4–8) should widen to Kenya (Wanjiru Koinange, Peter Kimani), South Africa (indie side of Kwela / NB Publishers), and diaspora indie authors publishing from London and New York.
- **Poetry, textbooks, kids' picture books, and academic titles are excluded on purpose.** Different economics, different reader, different beachhead. Come back to each as separate initiatives after the core catalog is proven.
